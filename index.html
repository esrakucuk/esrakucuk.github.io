About

I study how infants and young children come to hold concepts that experience alone could not have supplied, and how they represent and reason about uncertainty, possibility, and future outcomes.

I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the Harvard Graduate School of Education, working with Dr. Elizabeth Bonawitz. I completed my PhD in the Developmental Science Program at Boston University, where I worked with Dr. Melissa M. Kibbe on the conceptual and cognitive development of infants and young children.

My work asks one question in two domains: when a young child fails a task, has she not yet acquired the concept, or does she have it and lack the resources the task demands? I study this for the concept of possibility and for the concept of life, using behavioral experiments with 2- to 7-year-olds, infant looking-time methods, online testing, computational modeling, and Bayesian meta-analysis.

Research

Children’s concepts of possibility

What can young children represent as merely possible? Children succeed years earlier than classic tasks suggest when the uncertainty is about what an object is rather than where it is. I argue that what develops is not the concept itself but the representational format a task lets children use.

possibilityuncertaintyplanning

Children’s concepts of what is alive

How do children decide what is alive, tested on the hardest case, plants? I ask whether children’s expectations about plants run ahead of the judgments they are able to state out loud.
